**Research/evidence-synthesis only. No production code, no catalog mutation, no deterministic Appsel product values, no public rollout change.**

**Mandatory statement, per instruction, repeated verbatim as required: this report does not establish scientifically optimal Beginner prescriptions. It establishes evidence-compatible boundaries and constraints. GEN.4C will make deterministic Appsel product decisions inside those boundaries.**

## 3. Evidence methodology

Four-tier hierarchy applied throughout (Tier 1 direct scientific evidence → Tier 2 strong indirect scientific evidence → Tier 3 established coaching practice → Tier 4 Appsel-internal evidence, which proves feasibility/representability, never scientific optimality). Every claim classified `SUPPORTED`/`PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`/`INDIRECT`/`COACHING_PRACTICE_ONLY`/`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`/`CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE`/`UNSOURCED_HEURISTIC`. Web search was used to verify primary-source claims rather than relying on unverified recall (§33 lists exact sources retrieved). Where a claim could not be independently verified against a primary source, it is explicitly flagged rather than presented as established.

## 4. Source-quality rules

Applied per instruction: primary sources preferred for numeric claims; blog/commercial-summary content used only as Tier 3 coaching-practice evidence, never as scientific authority; observational association never converted to causal law; single-cohort thresholds never converted to universal hard rules.

## 5. Beginner evidence-population definition

**`BEGINNER_EVIDENCE_POPULATION_DEFINITION`**: GEN.4A confirmed `Beginner` = catalog `"NEW"` lineage, tracing back to the pre-V2 `NewToRunning` enum value. Existing Appsel onboarding semantics do not define Beginner as "completely sedentary/never exercised/unable to run continuously" — no such restriction was found in the inspected request/onboarding contracts during GEN.0/GEN.4. The most defensible research-population proxy, absent a stricter internal definition, is: **adults new to structured running training, who may have some general physical activity background but limited or no recent sustained running experience**, consistent with how "novice runner" is operationally defined in the running-injury-epidemiology literature (typically: began running training within the past 1-12 months, or resuming after a long gap).

**`EXCLUDED_OR_INDIRECT_POPULATIONS`**: trained recreational runners, competitive/collegiate runners, Advanced athletes, and clinical rehabilitation populations are excluded as direct evidence sources; they may be used only as explicitly-labeled indirect/comparative evidence (e.g., contrasting novice vs. recreational injury incidence, §7).

**`EVIDENCE_TRANSFER_LIMITATIONS`**: most running-injury cohorts recruit self-selected participants already engaged in a structured novice program (e.g., NLstart2run-style cohorts), which may not fully represent Appsel's actual Beginner population (who may arrive via an app with no coach oversight and variable adherence). This limitation applies to every Tier 1/Tier 2 claim below and is not repeated per-claim.

## 6. Observed starting-volume evidence

**Classification: `OBSERVED_VOLUME_DIRECTLY_USABLE`, with `OBSERVED_VOLUME_REQUIRES_SAFEGUARD` as a secondary, narrower finding.**

No evidence was found supporting a **systematic Beginner-specific discount** of a genuinely observed, valid `RecentWeeklyVolume` value. If a user self-identifies as Beginner but reports real recent running volume (e.g., 20km/week), that volume represents actual demonstrated capacity — discounting it "because Level says Beginner" would discard real user evidence in favor of a label, contradicting GEN.4A's frozen `OBSERVED_RECENT_VOLUME_IS_USER_EVIDENCE_AUTHORITY` invariant. This is confirmed as an architecture position (§14 of GEN.4A), not merely evidence — but the evidence base does not contradict it either: no source reviewed establishes that self-reported recent volume becomes systematically unreliable specifically because the reporting individual is a "Beginner" by product-Level classification.

The narrower `OBSERVED_VOLUME_REQUIRES_SAFEGUARD` finding is that **any** transition from an observed recent-volume baseline into a new progression trajectory (regardless of Level) already carries elevated risk if the *rate* of subsequent increase is aggressive — this is a progression-rate question (§9-10), not a starting-volume-authority question, and should not be conflated with discounting the observed value itself. `SUPPORTED` for "progression rate matters independent of starting point," `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` for "the starting-volume observation itself should be discounted for Beginners."

## 7. Missing starting-volume evidence

Beginner, 4D, TEN_K preparation, `RecentWeeklyVolume` unknown/not-provided.

**LOWER_BOUND_CANDIDATE**: bounded below by Appsel's own 4D structural mathematical floor (§12) — a starting weekly volume that cannot even minimally populate the RUN_LAYOUT_4D role structure (1 KEY + 2 EASY + 1 LONG) at each role's technical minimum is not representable at all, independent of any scientific question. Derived: **≈8.5–10.0 km/week** (see §12 derivation). Classification: `APPSEL_MATHEMATICAL_FLOOR`.

**PREFERRED_EVIDENCE_ENVELOPE**: no Tier 1/Tier 2 source establishes a precise "starting weekly volume for a novice entering 4-day/week structured 10K training." The closest indirect evidence is the general injury-epidemiology finding that novice runners carry markedly higher per-hour injury incidence than recreational runners (Kluitenberg et al., meta-analysis, 13 studies: novice 17.8/1000h [95% CI 16.7–19.1] vs. recreational 7.7/1000h [95% CI 6.9–8.7] — `SUPPORTED`, Tier 2, indirect to 10K-specific prescription but directly established for the novice-runner population generally). This supports **conservatism in general** but does not itself yield a km/week number. Tier 3 coaching-practice convergence (multiple independently-authored "Couch to 10K" program structures, cross-referenced during search) commonly describes early-week total volumes in a materially lower range than Appsel's current Intermediate 4D missing-fallback default of 16km/week, typically built around run-walk interval structures rather than continuous running from week 1. **Classification: `COACHING_PRACTICE_ONLY` for any specific number; `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` for a scientifically-derived point value.** A defensible **evidence-compatible envelope**, bounded below by the structural floor and above by Intermediate's own missing-fallback value (a Beginner default exceeding the Intermediate default would be internally incoherent), is approximately **9–16 km/week**, with coaching-tier sources clustering toward the lower half of that range.

## 8. Explicit-zero starting-volume evidence

**`EXPLICIT_ZERO_EVIDENCE_ENVELOPE`**: narrower than §7's missing-readiness envelope by definition (explicit zero signals materially less confidence than "unknown"). Bounded below by the same §12 structural floor (~8.5-10km) and above by Intermediate's own explicit-zero fallback (12 km/week), for the same parity-inference reasoning as §7.

**Full-4D-week-from-week-1 assessment**: `FULL_4D_WEEK_REQUIRES_READINESS_QUALIFICATION`. General exercise-prescription guidance for previously-inactive/novice adults (ACSM-framework-style progression for sedentary adults — Tier 3, general exercise-science convention, not 10K-running-specific) favors building continuous aerobic tolerance gradually rather than assuming full structured-session capacity from day one; this is corroborated indirectly by the elevated novice injury-incidence finding (§7). However, **RUN_LAYOUT_4D's structural cardinality (1 KEY + 2 EASY + 1 LONG) is frozen and must not be reduced** (GEN.4A invariant, reaffirmed non-negotiable by this phase's own non-goals). The evidence therefore bears on **what content/dosage fills each of the four roles in an explicit-zero week**, not on whether four roles can exist — reducing to `PRODUCT_ELIGIBILITY_DECISION_REQUIRED` for whether an explicit-zero Beginner should even be routed into a full 4D week at all (an eligibility question, addressed structurally in §16, not resolved here) versus `FULL_4D_WEEK_FROM_WEEK_1_BROADLY_EVIDENCE_COMPATIBLE` for the pure structural question (four low-dosage sessions, e.g. short run-walk EASY sessions plus a modest LONG, are not inherently evidence-incompatible — Tier 3 coaching precedent for exactly this shape is common). **No structural mutation (reducing to 3D, deleting a role, or manufacturing a synthetic short fourth session purely to preserve cardinality) is proposed or supported.**

## 9. Progression evidence

**Weekly relative/absolute progression.** The "10% rule" is explicitly **not** treated as scientific law, per instruction. Search evidence confirms this framing is contested in the literature: one commonly-cited finding is that runners increasing weekly distance by 20-60% showed significantly higher injury rates than those increasing by <20% — a much coarser, less precise dose-response signal than a crisp "10%" threshold implies (`PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`, Tier 2, indirect — population and injury-definition heterogeneity across the underlying studies was not independently verified in this pass and should be treated as a real limitation).

**Absolute weekly increase / single-session safeguard**: covered separately in §10-11 (Frandsen 2025).

**Beginner-specific progression evidence**: `NO_BEGINNER_SPECIFIC_PROGRESSION-RATE_EVIDENCE` — no source reviewed establishes a *precise* Beginner-specific percentage distinct from Intermediate's existing conservative values (`PreferredMaxWeeklyIncreaseRatio=0.07`, `HardMaxWeeklyIncreaseRatio=0.08`). This is stated explicitly rather than manufacturing a difference, per instruction. What **is** indirectly supported (`PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`, Tier 2) is that novice runners' materially higher baseline injury incidence (§7) provides general support for **not exceeding** Intermediate's already-conservative existing ratios, and plausibly for erring toward the more conservative end of any range GEN.4C ultimately selects — but this is a directional consideration, not a numeric derivation, and does not itself justify a *different* ratio.

## 10. Frandsen 2025 — Beginner transferability check

**Source verified**: Frandsen et al., 2025, British Journal of Sports Medicine — "How much running is too much? Identifying high-risk running sessions in a 5200-person cohort study" (Aarhus University-affiliated; also indexed via Luxembourg Institute of Health). **Classification: `SUPPORTED`, Tier 1** for the core finding as stated: a significant increase in running-related overuse injury rate was observed when a single session's distance exceeded the runner's own longest run in the preceding 30 days by a defined margin (search-verified framing: exceeding ~110% of the prior-30-day longest run was associated with sharply increased risk). **Not independently re-derived from the primary paper's full statistical tables in this pass** — the exact effect size, confidence interval, and dose-response shape beyond the headline threshold were not verified beyond the search-result summaries retrieved; GEN.4C or a dedicated follow-up should consult the primary BJSM publication directly before encoding any specific percentage.

**Explicit reinterpretation, not carried over unexamined from GEN.2B.1**: the finding concerns **relative single-session distance versus the runner's own recent longest run**, not a rule about weekly total mileage. This is preserved correctly here, consistent with the phase's explicit caution against overgeneralizing it into "weekly mileage must never increase by >10%."

**Beginner-transferability assessment**: the retrieved cohort description (5,200 runners) was not confirmed in this pass to report the cohort's experience-level distribution, baseline running status, or what fraction were novice versus trained. This is a genuine, disclosed evidence gap. Two structural concerns specific to Beginner transferability were identified independent of cohort composition: (1) the **low-baseline problem** — if a Beginner's prior-30-day longest run is 0 km or very small (e.g., 1-2 km), a *relative* percentage safeguard becomes mathematically unstable (110% of 0 is 0; 110% of 2km is 2.2km, a trivial and possibly meaningless constraint) or operationally meaningless as a real safety gate; (2) whether the *relative* spike-risk relationship holds with the same shape at very low absolute baseline load, versus being validated primarily in a mid-to-higher-baseline range, cannot be determined from the evidence retrieved in this pass.

**Classification: `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE_AT_LOW_BASELINE`.** The general safeguard mechanism (relative-to-recent-longest-run) is plausibly still directionally sound for Beginner, but its behavior at or near a zero/near-zero baseline requires either (a) a documented floor/minimum-denominator rule (an Appsel product decision, not a scientific one) or (b) explicit acknowledgment that the safeguard is not meaningfully load-bearing until some minimum longest-run baseline has been established. **Whether Beginner status justifies a lower operational spike threshold than Intermediate's is explicitly not assumed** — no evidence for or against a *different* threshold was found; the honest classification is `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE_AT_LOW_BASELINE`, not `BEGINNER_SPECIFIC_THRESHOLD_EVIDENCE_FOUND`.

## 12. 4D technical/evidence floor analysis

**`BEGINNER_4D_CURRENT_TECHNICAL_FLOOR`** (Tier 4, derived from real repository constants, `FourDaySessionDistanceAllocationPolicy`/`V1FourDaySessionVolumeAllocationPolicy`/`VolumeSafetyPolicy.Default`):

- Residual-only floor (KEY+EASY+EASY): `MinimumKeySessionDistanceKm(3) + 2×MinimumEasySupportDistanceKm(1.5) = 6.0 km`. **Classification: `WORKOUT_CATALOG_TECHNICAL_MINIMUM`** — confirmed hardcoded, role-based, Level-blind.
- Long-run minimum is **not** an independent absolute constant in the current 4D policy (unlike 3D's flat `MinimumLongKm=5`) — it is share-based (`LongRunPreferredMinimumShare=0.30` / `LongRunSelectionShare=0.33` / `LongRunHardCapShare=0.40`, all from `VolumeSafetyPolicy.Default`). Deriving a combined full-layout floor requires solving `weeklyVolume × (1 − longRunShare) ≥ 6.0`:
  - At `LongRunPreferredMinimumShare=0.30`: `weeklyVolume ≥ 6.0/0.70 ≈ 8.57 km` → rounds to **8.5 km** (per the 0.5km rounding rule).
  - At `LongRunSelectionShare=0.33`: `weeklyVolume ≥ 6.0/0.67 ≈ 8.96 km` → **9.0 km**.
  - At `LongRunHardCapShare=0.40`: `weeklyVolume ≥ 6.0/0.60 = 10.0 km`.
  - **`BEGINNER_4D_CURRENT_TECHNICAL_FLOOR` ≈ 8.5–10.0 km/week, depending on which specific share the taper-week (or any given week's) long-run computation actually resolves to** — this range, not a single point, is carried forward into §21's stress matrix. **This derivation is presented as an explicit mathematical consequence of already-existing constants, not a new architectural proposal**, but it was not independently confirmed against the exact taper-week long-run-selection code path in this pass and should be verified precisely before GEN.4C treats it as load-bearing.

**`BEGINNER_4D_EVIDENCE_SUPPORTED_FLOOR_RANGE`**: no direct scientific evidence establishes a *minimum viable* weekly volume for a 4-session structured novice week independent of Appsel's own catalog technical minimums — this question is answered entirely by Tier 4 (Appsel-internal) evidence today. **These are not claimed to be identical**, per instruction — the technical floor is a hard representability constraint; a genuinely evidence-supported "minimum useful training stimulus" floor, if one exists in the literature, was not located in this pass and is recorded as `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.

Classification of each floor component:

| KEY minimum (3.0 km) | `WORKOUT_CATALOG_TECHNICAL_MINIMUM` (currently `INTERMEDIATE_PRODUCT_DEFAULT_ONLY` in provenance — not independently evidenced for Beginner) |

## 16. One-KEY eligibility

**Classification: `ONE_KEY_REQUIRES_DOSAGE_CONSTRAINT`.**

The question posed is narrow and answered without proposing any structural change: can Beginner support the *one* KEY session RUN_LAYOUT_4D already requires? General evidence (§7, §9) supports that novice runners tolerate *some* structured-intensity exposure, but with materially more caution around volume/intensity concentration than more experienced runners — this argues for **dosage-constrained** eligibility (a real KEY session occurs, but at reduced/modified dosage and/or deferred until after an initial Foundation-equivalent period), not for eligibility being categorically absent (`NOT_SUPPORTED`) nor unconstrained (`ONE_KEY_BEGINNER_ELIGIBLE` outright). This is consistent with, and does not contradict, `LEVEL_NEVER_OVERRIDES_RUNLAYOUT_KEY_CARDINALITY` — the KEY slot exists structurally regardless; only its *content/dosage* is in question, and that is squarely a Level-eligibility/dosage decision (§14-15), not a structural one. **`ONE_KEY_REQUIRES_PHASE_CONSTRAINT` is also plausible** (deferring meaningful KEY-workout intensity until after Foundation) and is not distinguished from the dosage-constraint framing by evidence available in this pass — both are recorded as live candidates for GEN.4C, not resolved between here.

## 17. Session-allocation null-hypothesis result

**H0**: Beginner×4D uses the same structural KEY/EASY/EASY/LONG weekly-distance allocation authority as other 4D plans; only total weekly load differs.

**Classification: `NO_LEVEL_SPECIFIC_ALLOCATION_EVIDENCE`.**

No source reviewed in this pass (Tier 1, 2, or 3) provided a specific, Beginner-differentiated formula for how weekly volume should be *split* across KEY/EASY/EASY/LONG roles, as distinct from how *much total volume* should exist. The general injury/intensity-concentration concerns raised throughout (§9, §14) bear on **total load and intensity exposure**, which is captured by starting-volume/progression/peak-volume envelopes and by workout-dosage decisions (§15) — not by the *proportional split* mechanism itself. **The evidence located in this pass supports retaining Frequency ownership of the allocation mechanism for GEN.4C's consideration** — this phase does not formally approve that reuse (per instruction), it reports that no contradicting evidence was found.

## 18. Long-run null-hypothesis result

**H0**: Beginner×4D can use the same 4D long-run share authority, with safety still constrained by recent-longest-run/readiness evidence.

**Classification: `NO_LEVEL_SPECIFIC_LONG_RUN_EVIDENCE`.**

No source located a Beginner-specific long-run-share percentage distinct from Intermediate's `LongRunPreferredMinimumShare`/`Maximum`/`SelectionShare`/`HardCapShare`. The relevant safety concern for long-run prescription specifically — concentration of load into a single session, and the risk of a single-session spike — is already the exact concern Frandsen 2025's safeguard mechanism addresses (§10), which operates **independently of the long-run-share percentage** (it constrains absolute session distance relative to recent longest run, not the *proportion* of weekly volume the long run represents). No evidence was found that the share-percentage mechanism itself needs to differ for Beginner; the low-volume rounding-effects concern raised by the phase is addressed by §12's floor derivation, not by needing a different share formula. **Evidence supports retaining Frequency ownership for GEN.4C's consideration**, not formally approved here.

## 19. Recent-longest-run safeguard

Covered in depth in §10. Summary for this section's index: the safeguard mechanism (relative to prior-30-day longest run) is a real, evidence-grounded (Tier 1, Frandsen 2025) constraint, but its behavior at or near a zero/near-zero baseline (the explicit-zero Beginner scenario, §8) is mathematically unstable and not resolved by the evidence retrieved — `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE_AT_LOW_BASELINE`, requiring either a documented minimum-denominator floor rule or explicit acknowledgment of reduced safeguard efficacy at very low baselines. This is a **product-operationalization question for GEN.4C**, not resolved here.

## 20. Four-run frequency tolerance

**Classification: `BEGINNER_4D_REQUIRES_READINESS_QUALIFICATION`** (a blend of `BEGINNER_4D_FREQUENCY_BROADLY_SUPPORTED` for well-prepared Beginners and `BEGINNER_4D_REQUIRES_PRODUCT_ELIGIBILITY_DECISION` for low-readiness ones — the evidence does not support a single uniform answer).

Four running days/week is not, by itself, an unusual or evidence-incompatible frequency for novice 10K training — most coaching-tier program structures reviewed use 3-5 sessions/week, with 4 being common. However, the evidence on novice injury risk (§7) and the general exercise-prescription convention of building frequency/duration tolerance gradually (§8) together suggest that a Beginner with **very low recent running frequency or volume** (i.e., someone whose `RecentRunsPerWeek`/`RecentWeeklyVolume` signals near-zero recent activity) may not be equally well-served entering a full 4-day structured week immediately, compared to a Beginner with some existing activity base. **Prior volume is more directly informative than prior run count alone** — a person who runs once a week for 15km carries more demonstrated aerobic capacity than one who runs four times a week for 4km total, so frequency tolerance should not be assessed by run-count alone if volume evidence is available (this is a reasoned inference from the existing evidence about load/capacity, not itself directly sourced — flagged `UNSOURCED_HEURISTIC` for the specific "volume is more informative than count" framing, though the underlying capacity-based reasoning is consistent with general training-load principles). Whether Appsel should gate 4D eligibility for some low-readiness Beginners (vs. always allowing it with appropriately conservative dosage) is explicitly a **product-eligibility decision**, not resolved by evidence alone — recorded as `EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` to determine the gate threshold, consistent with the phase's own instruction not to choose one.

## 29. Source conflicts

The primary tension identified is between the popularly-cited "10% rule" (a precise, memorable heuristic) and the more nuanced injury-progression literature (which describes a coarser, less precise relationship — e.g., the 20-60% vs. <20% comparison in C3, and Frandsen's session-relative rather than week-relative framing in C2). These are not directly reconcilable into one precise number: the population differences (varied recreational cohorts vs. Frandsen's specific 5,200-runner cohort), load-metric definitions (weekly distance vs. single-session-relative-to-recent-longest-run), and study designs (retrospective/prospective mixed) all differ. **No mechanical averaging was performed.** The narrowest defensible envelope produced is: *retain a conservative weekly-progression ceiling (in the same general range as Intermediate's existing 7-8%, absent evidence for a precise different Beginner number) and rely primarily on the session-relative Frandsen mechanism as the more directly-evidenced single-session safeguard*, with the explicit caveat that the session-relative mechanism's low-baseline behavior is itself unresolved (`DECISION_REQUIRED_WITH_WEAK_EVIDENCE` for that specific sub-question).
