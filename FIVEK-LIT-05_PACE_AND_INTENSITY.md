# FIVEK-LIT-05 — Pace and Intensity Role for Canonical FIVE_K

**Phase:** FIVE-K.0A. **Maps to FIVE-K.0 blocker:** FK0-B5 (§47). **Research order:** 2nd (after FIVEK-LIT-04's structure, before volume/long-run/taper, per governing-prompt §6).

## 1. Research question

What is FIVE_K's own intensity architecture: the role of 5K race pace, threshold, VO2/interval, easy, and (if relevant) faster-than-5K/repetition pace — including phase placement, relative frequency, and whether continuous or interval format applies — and does the existing generic fitness-evidence hierarchy remain valid with no FIVE_K-specific contradiction?

## 2. Runtime authority being sought

FIVE_K-specific pace-role classification analogous to how TEN_K/HM bind target-race pace, threshold, interval, and easy roles into their own workout-progression and prescription-profile content; a V1-scope decision on whether shorter/faster-than-5K work is required.

## 3. Existing internal evidence already reviewed

FIVE-K.0 §27-28: the Riegel-based cross-distance pace-*projection* mechanism (`GoalFeasibilityResolver`) and the fitness-evidence hierarchy (`PaceSourceResolver`: recent-race → time-trial → structured-test → normal-pace → effort-fallback) are both `CLOSED_SHARED_AUTHORITY`, distance-agnostic, dormant/unwired regardless of distance. What remained silent was FIVE_K's own threshold/VO2/interval/easy *role* classification. This phase (FIVE-K.0A) reviewed the catalog's existing workout-family vocabulary directly (`plan-catalog/catalog/workouts/`): `EASY` (easy-standard), `LONG_RUN` (long-run-standard), and `QUALITY` (threshold-tempo, fartlek, goal-pace-ten-k, hm-pace, aerobic-strength-controlled-{intro,progressed}) — no dedicated VO2max/repetition-interval workout definition and no `goal-pace-five-k` workout exist anywhere in the catalog today.

## 4. External sources

Jack Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula* (interpreted via multiple independent secondary descriptions, as in LIT-04 — not page-verified): Repetition pace (faster than 5K/mile-race pace, short reps with full recovery, aimed at speed/economy) in Phase II; Threshold/cruise-interval pace in Phase II; Interval pace (~3K-5K race effort) in Phase III, used for 800-1200m-style reps. Luke Humphrey/Hansons 5K philosophy (lukehumphreyrunning.com, direct): progression "LT repeats and 10K-pace work... down to 5K, 3K, and even some mile-pace work"; explicit statement that "the 5K is largely aerobic... aerobic base is key." Midgley, McNaughton & Wilkinson (and related peer-reviewed VO2max-training literature, PubMed 9927024 and associated reviews, interpreted via abstract/summary-level secondary description, not full-text page citation): total time spent at or near VO2max is the strongest predictor of aerobic-ceiling improvement; 3-5 minute work bouts at velocity-at-VO2max are an efficient means of accumulating that time; work:rest of roughly 15-30s short-interval formats are also studied for a different (short-interval) VO2max protocol, distinct from the longer-rep format. Billat (interpreted via secondary description): velocity-at-VO2max (vVO2max) "tracks almost perfectly" with 3K-5K performance, i.e., VO2max-pace training is directly relevant to 5K-specific fitness, not merely general aerobic development. 80/20 Endurance / polarized-training literature (runculture.com, pheidi.training, 8020endurance.com — mixed primary/secondary; polarized-vs-pyramidal debate is itself an active, contested research area, see §6): roughly 80% low-intensity / 20% high-intensity volume split is described as "scientific consensus" by some secondary sources for elite endurance athletes generally, with explicit note that "5K-10K-focused runners are good fits for polarized training... shift to faster, shorter intervals and race-pace simulations during the peak phase."

## 5. Atomic claims, supporting evidence, classification

**Claim A — 5K race pace itself functions as a distinct, race-specific intensity tier, materially faster than threshold and roughly at or near (but evidence varies on exact relationship to) each individual's VO2max/interval pace, used specifically in the race-specific phase close to the goal race, not as the primary training stimulus throughout the cycle.** Daniels' own tiering (Repetition faster than Interval faster than Threshold faster than Easy) and Hansons' explicit "progress down to 5K... pace" late in the plan both place 5K pace near the top of the intensity hierarchy and late in sequencing. **Classification: STRONG_MULTI_SOURCE_SYNTHESIS.**

**Claim B — Threshold/tempo work is foundational/build-stage, not race-specific, for canonical 5K prep — it precedes VO2/interval and race-pace work rather than replacing it.** Daniels (Threshold in Phase II, before Phase III's Interval/race-pace work) and Hansons ("start out with more LT repeats... progress down to 5K") agree directionally. **Classification: STRONG_MULTI_SOURCE_SYNTHESIS.** Maps cleanly onto the existing catalog's `threshold-tempo` workout (already `QUALITY` family) — **EXISTING_WORKOUT_SUPPORTS_AUTHORITY**.

**Claim C — VO2max/interval-oriented work is a central, not optional, progression component for 5K preparation, with a well-established physiological rationale (vVO2max tracking closely with 3K-5K performance) distinct from generic "get fitter" training.** Billat and the Midgley/McNaughton VO2max literature (peer-reviewed, trained-athlete populations) converge with the training-text sources (Daniels Phase III, Hansons' stated progression toward 3K/5K/mile pace) on VO2max-proximate work being central, not a secondary-quality add-on, for 5K preparation specifically — distinguishing 5K from, e.g., marathon preparation, where such work plays a much smaller role. **Classification: STRONG_MULTI_SOURCE_SYNTHESIS** for centrality and phase placement (race-specific phase, following threshold work); **SOURCE_SILENCE** for any athlete-specific pace number (none is asserted here, consistent with governing-prompt §25's prohibition).

**Claim D — Shorter/faster-than-5K (repetition-pace) work is evidence-supported as a real, named element of structured 5K plans at least at the intermediate/advanced published level (Daniels' Repetition pace, Hansons' "mile-pace work sprinkled in"), but is not uniformly present across all cited sources at all levels** — Higdon's plans (all three levels) do not explicitly name a repetition/mile-pace workout category as distinct from the tempo/400m-repeat sessions already in its Intermediate/Advanced plans. **Classification for V1 requirement: OPTIONAL_SUPPORTED, not CANONICAL_REQUIRED** — multiple reputable sources include it at higher levels, but at least one reputable, widely-used source (Higdon, spanning all three levels) ships a complete, coherent 5K plan without a clearly separate repetition/faster-than-5K tier. Per governing-prompt §29 ("avoid unnecessary V1 complexity"), faster-than-5K/repetition work is not required to be modeled as its own stage for a minimum-viable canonical FIVE_K V1; it can be deferred to a later expansion without blocking V1. Catalog fit: no existing workout definition represents true short-repetition/VO2max-interval structure distinctly from `fartlek` (unstructured) or `threshold-tempo` (continuous, sub-maximal) — **NEW_WORKOUT_WOULD_BE_REQUIRED** if/when this tier is implemented; **CATALOG_SEMANTIC_MISMATCH** if `fartlek` were reused as a stand-in for structured VO2max intervals, since `fartlek` is unstructured/self-paced by design while VO2max-interval protocols in the literature specify fixed work/rest durations.

**Claim E — Easy running is the dominant volume category, consistent with the polarized/80-20 framing and with every training-text source's own weekly structure (Daniels: "rest of the days are E running"; Hansons: "5K is largely aerobic... aerobic base is key").** **Classification: STRONG_MULTI_SOURCE_SYNTHESIS.** Maps directly onto the existing `EASY`/`easy-standard` catalog family — **EXISTING_WORKOUT_SUPPORTS_AUTHORITY**.

**Claim F — Whether FIVE_K training should follow a strict 80/20 polarized intensity distribution specifically (as a quantitative target) is CONTESTED in the current literature, not settled.** The polarized-training search itself surfaced an explicitly adversarial secondary source ("New Study Strikes Fatal Blow to 80/20 Training Philosophy," 8020endurance.com) alongside sources describing it as "scientific consensus" for elite athletes. This is a genuine, live scientific disagreement about an exact quantitative intensity-distribution rule, not merely a population/scope difference. **Classification: CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE** for any exact 80/20 (or other specific ratio) quantitative rule; **STRONG_MULTI_SOURCE_SYNTHESIS** only for the qualitative, uncontested claim that "the large majority of weekly volume in 5K preparation is easy/low-intensity running, with a minority at threshold/VO2/race-pace intensity" — no exact percentage split is asserted as canonical authority here.

**Claim G — The existing generic fitness-evidence hierarchy (recent-race → time-trial → structured-test → normal-pace → effort-fallback) carries no FIVE_K-specific contradiction.** Nothing in any source reviewed proposes a different evidence hierarchy for a 5K goal specifically (e.g., no source says a 5K time trial should outrank a recent 10K race differently than the hierarchy already orders evidence types generically). **Classification: CLOSED_SHARED_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED** — retained as shared authority with no FIVE_K-specific override, consistent with FIVE-K.0 §28.

## 6. Contrary/limiting evidence

Claim F above is a genuine quantitative dispute, recorded as `CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE` rather than resolved. Separately, Daniels' Repetition-pace tier (faster than Interval) and the generic VO2max-interval literature's vVO2max framing are not perfectly identical constructs (Repetition pace in Daniels' own taxonomy is explicitly *faster* than VO2max/Interval pace, aimed at speed/economy rather than aerobic-ceiling development) — these were kept as two distinct claims (C and D) rather than merged, to avoid conflating VO2max-interval work with repetition/speed work.

## 7. Population applicability

Daniels/Hansons/Higdon: recreational-to-competitive 5K racers, matching Appsel's Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced population reasonably well. Billat/Midgley-McNaughton VO2max literature: trained endurance-athlete populations in controlled studies — directionally applicable to Intermediate/Advanced Appsel users, with lower confidence extending the *specific* physiological mechanism claims to true beginners who may not yet reliably produce a true VO2max effort in training; no numeric pace values were asserted from this literature regardless.

## 8. Source-silence notes

No FIVE_K-specific work:rest interval-structure ratio is asserted as canonical (governing-prompt §28 — not invented here). No exact quantitative easy:hard training-time split is asserted as settled (Claim F, conflicting).

## 9. Canonical implication

Threshold and easy roles map directly onto existing `QUALITY`/`threshold-tempo` and `EASY`/`easy-standard` catalog workouts with no new workout required. A FIVE_K-specific `goal-pace-five-k` race-pace workout (paralleling the existing `goal-pace-ten-k`) is architecturally a straightforward parametrization of the same `QUALITY` family pattern — **EXISTING_WORKOUT_SUPPORTS_AUTHORITY** by structural analogy, pending confirmation when FIVE-K.1 actually authors it (not authored in this phase). A dedicated VO2max/interval workout distinct from `fartlek` would be **NEW_WORKOUT_WOULD_BE_REQUIRED** for V1 if VO2max-interval work is included; since Claim D classifies faster-than-5K/repetition work as `OPTIONAL_SUPPORTED` rather than `CANONICAL_REQUIRED`, this new-workout need can be deferred without blocking a minimum V1 built primarily on existing `EASY`/`threshold-tempo`/`LONG_RUN` families plus a race-pace analog.

## 10. Product-default implication

Whether V1 includes a dedicated VO2max-interval or repetition-pace workout tier is a product scope decision, not a pure evidence question (evidence supports it as a legitimate, evidence-backed *option*, not as mandatory for a coherent plan). See phase report PD-FIVEK item for V1 workout-tier scope.
