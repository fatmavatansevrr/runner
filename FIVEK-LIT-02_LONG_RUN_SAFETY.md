# FIVEK-LIT-02 — Long-Run Safety Authority for Canonical FIVE_K

**Phase:** FIVE-K.0A. **Maps to FIVE-K.0 blocker:** FK0-B2 (§47). **Research order:** 4th (after structure, intensity, and overall volume, per governing-prompt §6).

## 1. Research question

What is FIVE_K's long-run role, preferred/hard-cap share of weekly volume, whether an absolute ceiling is warranted, starting-cap authority, and whether frequency (3D vs. 5D) materially changes long-run authority?

## 2. Runtime authority being sought

`LongRunPreferredMinimumShare`/`LongRunPreferredMaximumShare`/`LongRunSelectionShare`/`LongRunHardCapShare` values for FIVE_K (or confirmation the mechanism applies with FIVE_K-specific values); `PreferredAbsolutePeakLongRunKm` (nullable by design, per TEN_K's own precedent — not automatically required); `StartingAbsoluteLongRunCapKm`.

## 3. Existing internal evidence already reviewed

FIVE-K.0 §9/§25/§32: the long-run share-quadruple *mechanism* is `CLOSED_SHARED_AUTHORITY`; FIVE_K values were `SOURCE_SILENCE`. The absolute long-run ceiling was `SOURCE_SILENCE`, explicitly noting TEN_K's own real precedent of leaving `PreferredAbsolutePeakLongRunKm` null (share-based governance alone, confirmed sufficient for TEN_K by DERIVED-DIST.4A §11) is not copyable to FIVE_K without FIVE_K-specific evidence either way.

## 4. External sources

Multiple convergent secondary/tertiary sources on long-run share (runculture/strengthrunning/trailrunnermag/fleetfeet style guidance, and one explicit attribution to Jack Daniels via a secondary description): "runners should never exceed 25-30 percent of their weekly mileage in a long run, whether training for a 5K or a marathon" (attributed to Daniels via secondary description, not page-verified); independent secondary consensus describing "around 20-30% of weekly mileage" as a common long-run-share guideline. A 2:30-3:00 hour absolute *time* ceiling is cited in secondary guidance as applying "across different race distances" (i.e., general, not 5K-specific). Higdon's own Advanced 5K plan (direct source): weekly structure of 6 runs, longest run builds to 12 miles ≈ 19.3km / "90 minutes" at the peak week, within a 6-day/week plan. A separate secondary claim ("for advanced 5K runners, typical weekly training includes 5-6 runs with 1 long run of 7-10 miles, which should usually be 20-35% of weekly volume") was found in a lower-tier blog-style secondary source (vorlich.com) and is recorded as weaker-tier corroboration only.

## 5. Atomic claims, supporting evidence, classification

**Claim A — A long-run share in the broad 20-30% (up to ~35% in one weaker secondary source) range of weekly volume is a widely repeated general-distance-running guideline, explicitly described by at least one source as applying "whether training for a 5K or a marathon" — i.e., not itself a distance-differentiated rule.** This is important: the clearest, most-repeated share figure found is *explicitly general*, not FIVE_K-derived — it is evidence that a generic (not FIVE_K-owned) long-run-share convention exists and is commonly applied across distances including 5K, which is corroborating evidence for *using the existing generic mechanism* rather than evidence of a FIVE_K-unique number. **Classification: STRONG_MULTI_SOURCE_SYNTHESIS** for "a generic ~20-30% share convention is appropriate to apply to FIVE_K, with no identified reason it should differ," but this is evidence for *re-using the existing generic mechanism with a value in that general band*, not `DIRECT_CANONICAL_EVIDENCE` of a FIVE_K-unique number distinct from what any other distance would use.

**Claim B — No source proposes a FIVE_K-specific absolute long-run-ceiling kilometre figure.** Higdon's own Advanced 5K plan's peak long run (12 miles / 19.3km) is a real, directly observed data point from a reputable published plan, but it is a single-source, single-level observation (Advanced only), not independently corroborated by a second source at the same level with a comparable figure, and Higdon never frames it as a "ceiling" (a maximum not to exceed) — it is simply the peak week's prescribed long run within one specific published plan. **Classification: SOURCE_SILENCE** for a defensible FIVE_K-specific absolute ceiling value; **EVIDENCE_INFORMED_PRODUCT_DEFAULT_REQUIRED** at best, if a future phase decides a ceiling is wanted for Advanced specifically, informed by (not derived via scaling from) the Higdon data point.

**Claim C — Per governing-prompt §38/FIVE-K.0 §36 item 3's own open evidence question (whether TEN_K's own "share-only, no absolute ceiling" precedent is independently appropriate at 5K proportions): the evidence found supports that a share-only (no absolute ceiling) model IS plausible for FIVE_K, because 5K-race long-run absolute distances are mechanically smaller than 10K/HM long runs even at the same share percentage (a 30% share of a 40km week ≈ 12km, materially smaller in absolute terms than HM's governed 19.0km ceiling or even TEN_K's own un-ceilinged but larger-volume long runs) — i.e., the smaller total-volume context of 5K training structurally bounds long-run absolute size even without an explicit ceiling, more so than it does for longer target distances.** This is an *inference from the combination of* Claim A (share convention) and LIT-01's volume-band findings, not a direct single-source statement — it is reasoning, not a new primary source, and is disclosed as such. **Classification: EVIDENCE_INFORMED_PRODUCT_DEFAULT_REQUIRED** (the reasoning is sound and consistent with TEN_K's own real precedent, but it is this phase's own synthesis/inference, not an external-source-stated rule, so it cannot be elevated to `STRONG_MULTI_SOURCE_SYNTHESIS` or `DIRECT_CANONICAL_EVIDENCE`) — explicitly flagged for product-decision approval rather than silently adopted, per governing-prompt §53.

**Claim D — No source independently varies long-run share or ceiling by RunsPerWeek (3D vs. 5D) for 5K training specifically.** Higdon's Novice(3D)/Intermediate(5D)/Advanced(6D) plans conflate level and frequency (as in LIT-01 Claim C), so no isolated 3D-vs-5D-at-the-same-level comparison exists in any source reviewed. **Classification: SOURCE_SILENCE** for any frequency-specific long-run differentiation — consistent with governing-prompt §42's explicit instruction not to infer this from TEN_K/HM's own numeric patterns.

**Claim E — Starting/calibration long-run cap: no FIVE_K-specific authority found; same conclusion as LIT-01 Claim E (generic current-conditioning-based governance, already shared infrastructure, has no FIVE_K-specific contrary evidence).** **Classification: CLOSED_SHARED_AUTHORITY_CONFIRMED_BY_ABSENCE_OF_CONTRARY_EVIDENCE.**

## 6. Contrary/limiting evidence

The weaker secondary source's 20-35% band (vorlich.com) sits slightly above the more frequently repeated 20-30% figure; this is a minor range difference between a higher- and lower-tier source, not a true conflict — the higher-tier, more-repeated figure (20-30%, explicitly cross-distance) is weighted more heavily per the source-priority standard (governing-prompt §7), and the discrepancy is recorded as a tier/precision difference, not `CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE`.

## 7. Population applicability

The cross-distance 20-30% share guideline's population is unspecified/general in the secondary sources citing it (not restricted to elite or novice) — treated as broadly applicable to Appsel's recreational Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced population absent contrary evidence. Higdon's Advanced 12-mile long run reflects a "veteran runner" population per Higdon's own framing, not directly generalizable to Beginner/Intermediate without evidence, which is exactly why Claim B is not elevated beyond product-default-informing status.

## 8. Source-silence notes

No FIVE_K-specific absolute ceiling value (Claim B). No frequency-differentiated long-run authority (Claim D).

## 9. Canonical implication

The existing generic long-run-share mechanism can be parameterized for FIVE_K using the generic ~20-30% band already shown (by at least one source) to apply across distances including 5K — this is the strongest, most-defensible FIVE_K-relevant finding in this file. Whether to add an explicit absolute ceiling (particularly for Advanced, informed by but not copied from Higdon's 12-mile/19.3km observed peak) remains a product decision, not a closed evidence question.

## 10. Product-default implication

PD-shaped decision: adopt share-only governance (no absolute ceiling) for FIVE_K V1, consistent with the structural reasoning in Claim C and with TEN_K's own real precedent, OR adopt an explicit Advanced-tier ceiling informed by Higdon's single-source 19.3km data point. Both are legitimate options; neither is approved here. See phase report PD-FIVEK items.
